# US Naturalization (5-year rule)

## Overview

:::callout
Naturalizing normally means **5 years** as a lawful permanent resident, and two separate residence tests have to be
satisfied inside it. **Continuous residence** must be unbroken — an absence of 6 months or more can break it, and a year
or more almost always does. **Physical presence** must total at least **30 months**, half the period. Passing one and
failing the other is not enough.
:::

| Key facts               |                                                |
|:------------------------|-----------------------------------------------:|
| Absence limit           |          6 months breaks continuity |
| Qualifying period       |                     5 years as a green card holder |
| Period / Window         |             5 years back from application date |
| Alternative             |    30 months physical presence required |
| Additional requirements | 3-month state residence, good moral character |

## Understanding the rule

Two tests run in parallel over the same 5 years, and they measure different things:

- **Continuous residence** — whether your residence was unbroken. It's about the *shape* of your absences, and a single
  long trip can break it even if your total time abroad is modest.
- **Physical presence** — the arithmetic total of days actually in the US, which must reach **30 months** across the
  5 years. Many short trips can fail this while never breaking continuity.

For continuous residence, the thresholds work like this. An absence of **6 months to under a year** creates a
rebuttable presumption that continuity broke, which you can overcome with evidence you kept your US home, job and
ties. An absence of **1 year or more** breaks continuity outright in almost every case, and the clock then restarts —
you generally wait 4 years and a day from returning before you can apply again.

The route is shorter for spouses of US citizens: **3 years** as a permanent resident, **18 months** of physical
presence, living in marital union with the citizen spouse for the whole period.

Alongside residence you need **3 months** in the state or district where you file, and **good moral character** across
the qualifying period.

## How to keep track

1. Two counts run at once: total **physical presence** of at least 30 months, and **continuous residence** unbroken by
   any single long absence.
2. **Any part of a day** in the US counts toward physical presence, including arrival and departure days.
3. Watch each individual trip against the **6-month** line. Crossing it creates a presumption against you that has to
   be rebutted with evidence of maintained ties.
4. An absence of **1 year or more** breaks continuity in almost all cases, restarting the clock — normally 4 years and
   a day from your return.
5. The 5-year window counts **backwards from your application date**, and you may file up to 90 days early while still
   satisfying the residence period.

Keep every entry and exit date across the whole period, plus evidence of US ties for any trip approaching six months —
lease or mortgage, employment records, resident tax returns, family in the US.

## Edge cases

- **The two tests fail independently.** Someone who never leaves for more than a month can still miss 30 months of
  physical presence, and someone with plenty of presence can break continuity with one long trip.
- **A re-entry permit protects your green card, not your naturalization clock.** It preserves permanent residence
  during a long absence, but the same absence can still break continuous residence for citizenship purposes.
- **Filing taxes as a non-resident undermines both.** It's a formal statement that you don't reside in the US, and it
  contradicts the residence these tests are built on.
- **A specific application preserves continuity for some overseas work.** People employed abroad by qualifying US
  employers or organisations can apply to preserve continuous residence, but it must be applied for rather than
  assumed.
- **Good moral character is assessed across the qualifying period** and certain conduct bars it absolutely, entirely
  separately from any day count.

## If you get this rule wrong

A refusal on residence grounds usually means restarting rather than reapplying — after a year-long absence the wait is normally 4 years and a day from your return, so a single miscalculated trip can cost most of a fresh qualifying period. The application fee isn't refunded, and where the refusal touches good moral character or the accuracy of the application it reaches further, since misrepresentation can put your permanent residence itself at risk. **Professional immigration advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### Clearing both tests comfortably

You've held a green card for six years, live and work in the US, and travel abroad three or four weeks a year. Your
physical presence is far past 30 months and no single absence approaches six months, so both tests hold.

### Enough presence, broken continuity

Across five years you've spent 38 months in the US — well past 30 — but one seven-month trip abroad sits in the middle.
That trip presumes continuity broke, and unless you can rebut it with evidence of maintained ties, the application
fails.

### Continuity intact, presence short

You travel constantly for work, never away for more than five weeks at a time, so continuity is never in question. But
your days in the US across five years total around 26 months, short of the 30 required, so the presence test fails.

## FAQ

### What's the difference between continuous residence and physical presence?

Continuous residence is about whether any single absence broke your residence — 6 months creates a presumption, a year
almost always breaks it. Physical presence is the plain total of days in the US, which must reach 30 months across the
5 years.

### How long can I leave the US while my citizenship clock is running?

Under 6 months per trip is the safe zone. Between 6 months and a year, you're presumed to have broken continuity and
have to rebut it. A year or more breaks it in almost every case.

### What happens if I break continuous residence?

The clock restarts. After an absence of a year or more you generally have to wait 4 years and a day from your return
before applying again.

### Is it faster if I'm married to a US citizen?

Yes. The period drops to 3 years as a permanent resident with 18 months of physical presence, provided you've lived in
marital union with your citizen spouse throughout.

### Does a re-entry permit protect my citizenship application?

No. It protects your green card during a long absence, but that absence can still break continuous residence for
naturalization. They're separate tests with separate consequences.

### When can I actually file?

You may file up to 90 days before completing the residence period, so the application can go in shortly before your
5 years — or 3 years on the spouse route — is technically complete.
