# Chile Tax Residency (183-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
You're a Chilean tax resident if you spend **more than 183 days** in Chile, continuous or not, within any twelve-month
period. You can also become resident through **domicile** — living in Chile with the intention of staying — which needs
no day count at all and can apply from your first day. New foreign residents get a transitional period before their
foreign income is taxed in Chile.
:::

| Key facts               |                             |
|:------------------------|----------------------------:|
| Thresholds              |                    184 days |
| Period / Window         | Any rolling 12-month period |
| Counting                |          Any part of a day  |
| Additional requirements |               Domicile test |

## Understanding the rule

You are a Chilean tax resident if you meet either of two tests, in any order:

- **Residence** — you are present in Chile for more than 183 days, continuous or not, within any twelve-month period.
  Residence is acquired on the 184th day, and it turns purely on physical presence, not on why you were there.
- **Domicile** — you live in Chile with the intention of remaining, judged mainly by economics: where your principal
  business is run and where your main source of income sits. Family ties support the picture but don't decide it alone.

Domicile can apply from your first day in the country. Where you formally declare an intention to stay, in a sworn
declaration to the tax authority, you are treated as domiciled in Chile from the day you entered. No further evidence
and no day count are needed. The two tests are independent, so you can hold one without the other.

Either one makes you liable to Chilean tax on your worldwide income. There is an important transition for newcomers,
though. A foreigner who takes up domicile or residence in Chile is taxed only on Chilean-source income for the first
three years. That clock runs from the date of entry, not from the date residency was triggered, and the regional tax
director can extend it in qualified cases. Once it ends, worldwide taxation applies in full.

## How to keep track

1. The threshold is **more than 183 days** of presence in Chile, continuous or not, inside any twelve-month period —
   you become resident on day 184.
2. The window is **rolling**, not the calendar year. Any twelve consecutive months can trigger it, so the count doesn't
   reset on 1 January.
3. Days of entry and exit both count as full days present. Days spent only in transit through Chile, on a journey
   between two points outside the country, are left out of the count.
4. Residence is lost by being absent from Chile for at least 184 days, continuous or not, within a twelve-month period.
   Losing residence does not by itself end domicile — that has to be documented separately.
5. Keep travel records for your day count, evidence of where your main business and income sources sit for the domicile
   test, and proof of your entry date, since the three-year transition runs from it.

## Edge cases

- **Domicile can make you resident from day one.** A sworn declaration of an intention to stay is enough on its own to
  fix domicile at your date of entry, long before any day count matters.
- **Dropping below the day count doesn't automatically end your Chilean tax position.** Domicile survives on its own
  and has to be unwound with evidence that your principal business and income sources have genuinely left Chile.
- **The three-year clock runs from entry, not from the day you became resident.** Arriving early in a year and only
  crossing 183 days much later still consumes part of the window.
- **The transitional treatment can be extended.** The regional tax director has discretion to prolong the
  Chilean-source-only period in qualified cases, but it is a request, not an automatic entitlement.
- **Dual residency is possible.** If another country also treats you as resident, a double tax treaty's tie-breaker
  rules can assign a single treaty residence without cancelling your Chilean domestic status.

## If you get this rule wrong

An incomplete or incorrect return that under-assesses tax carries a fine of 5% to 20% of the difference, unless you can
show you took proper care. A return the tax authority treats as maliciously false carries 50% to 300% of the tax
evaded, alongside possible criminal charges. Chile has no standing amnesty, but a regional director can waive or reduce
a fine for a taxpayer who reports and confesses first — in practice, a declaración rectificatoria filed before the
authority asks. **Professional tax advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### Straight past the threshold

You arrive in Santiago in early March and stay put, spending 250 days in Chile over your first twelve months. You cross
day 184 in early September and are a Chilean tax resident from that date — though for your first three years you're
taxed only on your Chilean-source income.

### Transit days keep you under

You travel to Chile repeatedly for work, and your busiest twelve-month stretch shows 186 days on Chilean soil. Four of
those were spent only changing planes on the way between two foreign cities. Transit days don't count, leaving 182
countable days, so you stay under the threshold and remain a nonresident.

### Few days, but the business is Chilean

You spend just 90 days in Chile this year, but you run your company from Santiago, it is your main source of income,
and you've declared your intention to settle there. Your day count is nowhere near the threshold, but domicile makes
you a Chilean tax resident regardless.

## FAQ

### Is Chilean tax residency just a 183-day rule?

No. The day count is one route, but **domicile** is an entirely separate one. Living in Chile with the intention of
staying, with your principal business and main income source there, can make you resident with no day count at all.

### Does the Chilean day count reset each calendar year?

No. Chile uses **any rolling twelve-month period**, so the relevant window can start on any date. A stay that straddles
1 January is counted straight through rather than split into two annual totals.

### Do arrival, departure and transit days count?

Arrival and departure days both count as full days of presence. Days spent purely in transit through Chile, on a trip
between two places outside the country, are excluded from the total.

### Do new foreign residents pay Chilean tax on foreign income straight away?

No. A foreigner who establishes domicile or residence in Chile is taxed only on Chilean-source income for the first
three years, counted from the date of entry. The regional tax director can extend that period in qualified cases;
afterwards, worldwide income is taxed.

### How do I stop being a Chilean tax resident?

Residence ends after at least 184 days of absence, continuous or not, within a twelve-month period. Domicile is
separate and doesn't lapse automatically — you need evidence that your principal business and income sources have
genuinely moved out of Chile.

### Can I be a tax resident of Chile and another country at the same time?

Yes. Chilean rules only settle your position under Chilean law. If another country also claims you, a double tax treaty
between the two would decide which one takes priority for particular types of income.
