# Canada Visitor Visa

## Overview

:::callout
Most visitors to Canada are admitted for **6 months** from their entry date. If the officer doesn't stamp your passport
you're still admitted for 6 months. There is **no annual cap** and **no rolling window**.
:::

| Key parameters          |                                     |
|:------------------------|------------------------------------:|
| Limit                   |                            6 months |
| Period / Window         |                           Per visit |
| Counting                |                   Any part of a day |
| Additional requirements | eTA or visa by nationality, no work |

## Understanding the rule

Two separate questions decide your position, and mixing them up is the usual source of confusion.

**What you need to travel** turns on nationality. Visa-exempt nationals flying to Canada need an **electronic travel
authorization (eTA)**, valid up to 5 years or until the passport expires. Visa-required nationals need a **temporary
resident visa** applied for in advance. Arriving by land or sea, visa-exempt travelers generally need neither, though
the visa requirement still applies to those who need one. United States citizens sit outside both requirements
entirely, whichever way they travel.

**How long you can stay** is decided separately, at the border, by a Canada Border Services Agency officer. The default
is 6 months, but the officer can grant less and will mark the passport accordingly. The rule that catches people is the
opposite case: **no stamp means 6 months from the day you entered**, so an unstamped passport isn't open-ended — it's a
six-month clock you have to track yourself.

Visitor status carries no right to work and no right to study beyond very short courses. Both require their own
permits, applied for separately.

## How to keep track

1. The default is **6 months** from your date of entry. If the officer wrote a different date in your passport, that
   date governs instead.
2. **No stamp means 6 months from your entry date.** You can ask the officer for a stamp if you want the date recorded,
   which is worth doing if you plan to stay a while.
3. **Any part of a day** counts, so your arrival and departure days both fall inside the period.
4. To stay longer, apply for a **visitor record** — the document that extends your status. Apply at least 30 days
   before your current status expires, and stay in Canada while it's processed.
5. Applying before expiry gives you **implied status**: you can remain lawfully while the decision is pending, even
   past your original date.

Keep your eTA or visa, boarding passes, and any stamped date. If your passport wasn't stamped, keep your own record of
the entry date, because that date is what your six months runs from.

## Edge cases

- **An eTA's validity is not your stay limit.** A five-year eTA lets you keep travelling for five years, each arrival
  still subject to a six-month admission decided at the border.
- **A visitor record is not a visa.** It extends how long you may stay, but it doesn't let you re-enter — if you leave
  Canada, you need your original eTA or visa to come back.
- **Parents and grandparents can stay far longer.** A super visa lets the parent or grandparent of a Canadian citizen
  or permanent resident stay up to **5 years** per entry, extendable by up to 2 years from inside Canada. It carries
  its own medical and private health insurance conditions.
- **Border officers can admit you for less than 6 months.** A short admission is entirely within their discretion, and
  the date they write is the one that counts.

## If you get this rule wrong

Staying past your authorised date ends your status, and every later application is assessed against a record that now shows non-compliance. Enforcement leads to a removal order — most often an exclusion order, barring return for 1 year from the date it is enforced, or 5 years where it was made for misrepresentation. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A visit with no stamp

You fly into Toronto on an eTA and the officer waves you through without stamping your passport. Nothing in your
documents shows a date, but you're admitted until six months from that day, and it's on you to know when that falls.

### Extending with a visitor record

Four months into a six-month stay you decide to stay longer. You apply for a visitor record about a month before your
status expires, remain in Canada, and hold implied status while the application is decided.

### An eTA misread as permission to stay

You hold a five-year eTA and assume it covers a long stay. It only covers repeated travel over those five years — each
arrival still gets you six months at most, so a nine-month stay would put you out of status from month seven.

## FAQ

### How long can I stay in Canada as a visitor?

Usually 6 months from your date of entry. A border officer can grant less and will write the date in your passport, and
that written date governs if there is one.

### Is it 6 months or 180 days?

Six calendar months, counted from your entry date — not a flat 180 days. Enter on 16 February and you're admitted
until 16 August, which is 181 days. The "180-day rule" is a common misreading that costs you a few days.

### What if my passport wasn't stamped?

You're deemed admitted for 6 months from the day you entered. Nothing records it, so track the date yourself — or ask
the officer for a stamp when you arrive if you'd prefer it documented.

### What's the difference between an eTA and a visitor visa?

An eTA is for visa-exempt nationals flying to Canada and is obtained online. A visitor visa is for visa-required
nationals and is applied for in advance. Which one applies depends on your nationality.

### How do I stay longer than 6 months?

Apply for a visitor record before your current status expires — at least 30 days beforehand is recommended. Applying in
time gives you implied status, letting you remain lawfully while the decision is pending.

### Does a visitor record let me leave and come back?

No. It extends your stay but isn't a travel document. Re-entering Canada requires your original eTA or visitor visa.

### What if my status has already expired?

You may be able to apply to restore it within 90 days, staying in Canada throughout and paying the restoration fee.
After 90 days that option is gone and you're expected to leave.
