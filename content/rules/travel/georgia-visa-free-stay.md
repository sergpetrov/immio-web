# Georgia Visa-Free Stay (12-month & 90/180-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
Citizens of the countries on Georgia's list can enter and stay **12 full months (~365 days)** without a visa, counted
**from the day you cross the border**. Other visa-exempt travelers get **90 days in any 180-day period** instead.
Since **1 March 2026**, paid work needs a separate permission.
:::

| Key parameters          |                            |
|:------------------------|---------------------------:|
| Limit                   |      12 months (~365 days) |
| Period / Window         |                  Per visit |
| Counting                |          Any part of a day |
| Alternative             | 90 days / Rolling 180 days |
| Additional requirements | Approval for any paid work |

## Understanding the rule

This applies to citizens of the countries named on the government's visa-free list, and to holders of a United Nations
Laissez-Passer, who get the same 12 months.

The 12 months are an exception rather than the norm. A traveler who needs no short-term visa is otherwise limited to
**90 calendar days in any 180-day period**, and the list exists to give the nationalities on it a longer period instead.
The list is a live document, amended repeatedly since it was first approved, so check your own nationality against the
current version before booking.

Staying beyond the 12 months means moving onto something else — a residence permit, or an immigration visa applied for
while you are still lawfully in the country.

## How to keep track

1. The permission is **12 full months (~365 days)**, granted on entry and calculated from the **date you cross the border**.
   Georgia states it as **one full year** and publishes no day equivalent, so the entry date is what your deadline runs
   from.
2. There is **no annual allowance to ration** on this route: the 12 months attach to your entry rather than being
   measured backwards across a rolling window.
3. **Any part of a day** counts as a day of presence, so the days you arrive and leave both sit inside the permitted
   period.
4. If your nationality is **not on the list** but you still need no short-term visa, the limit is
   **90 calendar days in any 180-day period**, measured backwards from the day in question.
5. A residence permit has to be applied for **while you are still lawfully present**, so the application belongs
   inside the 12 months rather than after it.

Keep your entry stamps and boarding passes, and note the exact entry date on arrival — that single date, not the date
you booked or landed at your final destination, is what your permitted stay is measured from.

## Edge cases

- **Paid work now needs its own approval.** Since 1 March 2026, foreign nationals without a permanent residence permit
  who work for a Georgian employer, take a remote job with one, or are self-employed in Georgia need a right to work
  approved by the State Employment Support Agency. Working without it can be fined.
- **An unpaid fine blocks your return.** Entry can be refused to anyone banned from Georgia or who has not paid a fine
  imposed for an unlawful stay, so an old penalty follows you to the border.
- **Entry is never automatic.** Officers can refuse someone lacking the documents required for entry, or without
  health and accident insurance or sufficient funds for the stay and the journey home. A presence judged a threat to
  public order is a further ground.
- **The list changes by ordinance.** Countries have been added and removed since the list was approved, and an
  amendment takes effect on publication rather than on any notice period.
- **EU identity cards are accepted, but only for EU citizens.** The card has to carry the name, surname, date of birth
  and photograph. Everyone else needs a travel document.

## If you get this rule wrong

Overstaying is an administrative offense, and the penalties were raised sharply on 1 October 2025 so that a fine now comes with an entry ban attached. An overstay of less than 3 months carries a fine and a 6-month ban, one running up to 12 months a higher fine and a 2-year ban, and anything past 12 months the highest fine and a 3-year ban. Breaching the transit rules carries its own separate fine. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A long stay inside the 12 months

You fly into Tbilisi in April on a passport from a listed country and are admitted visa-free. Your permitted stay runs
a full 12 months from that entry date, so a 10-month trip needs nothing further.

### Planning past the 12 months

You arrive in September and want to stay indefinitely. Because the 12 months run from your entry date, the residence
permit application has to be made before it expires, while you are still lawfully in the country.

### Working during the visa-free stay

You take a job with a Tbilisi company while staying visa-free. The stay itself is lawful, but the work is not until
the employer has the right-to-work approval, and going without it exposes both of you to a fine.

## FAQ

### How long can I stay in Georgia without a visa?

12 full months if your nationality is on the government's list, counted from the date you cross the border. Travelers
who are visa-exempt but not on that list get 90 calendar days in any 180-day period.

### Do the 12 months restart if I leave and come back?

Your permitted stay is calculated from the date you cross the border, so a fresh entry starts a fresh period. Entry
itself is still decided by the officer, and a pattern that looks like residence rather than visiting can be refused.

### Can I work in Georgia during the visa-free stay?

Not without approval. Since 1 March 2026 a right to work from the State Employment Support Agency is needed by anyone
without a permanent residence permit who works for a Georgian employer or is self-employed in Georgia.

### What happens if I overstay in Georgia?

A fine, and an entry ban that scales with how long you overstayed. The fine also has to be paid before you will be let
back in, because an unpaid one is itself a ground for refusing entry.

### Can EU citizens enter Georgia with an ID card?

Yes. Citizens of EU member states can travel on a national identity card showing name, surname, date of birth and a
photograph, rather than a passport.

### How do I stay in Georgia longer than 12 months?

By obtaining a residence permit, or an immigration visa applied for while you are lawfully in the country. Both are
decided on their own conditions, and neither is an extension of the visa-free stay.
