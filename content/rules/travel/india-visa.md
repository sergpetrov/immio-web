# India e-Tourist Visa (180-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
**180 days per calendar year** is the real cap on India's e-Tourist visa, whichever version you hold. A single visit
is capped separately — **90 days** for most nationalities, **180 days** for US, UK, Canada and Japan citizens. The
visa comes in **30-day**, **1-year** and **5-year** versions.
:::

| Key parameters          |                                     |
|:------------------------|------------------------------------:|
| Limit                   |                            180 days |
| Period / Window         |                           Per visit |
| Counting                |                   Any part of a day |
| Alternative             |                   90 days per visit |
| Additional requirements | 180 days per calendar year, no work |

## Understanding the rule

The three main variants are different permissions, not just different lengths of the same one:

- **30-day** — valid 30 days from your first arrival, multiple entries, no annual cap.
- **1-year** — valid 365 days from grant, multiple entries, capped at 180 days of stay per calendar year.
- **5-year** — valid 5 years from grant, multiple entries, the same 180-day annual cap.

A **single visit** is also capped, separately from the annual total: 90 days for most nationalities — though citizens
of the **United States, United Kingdom, Canada and Japan** get **180 days** per visit instead, enough to use a full
year's allowance in one trip.

The e-Visa permits tourism, visiting family, short yoga or medical programmes, and casual business meetings under the
separate business e-Visa — not employment, journalism, research, or missionary work.

## How to keep track

1. On the 1-year and 5-year visas, the limit is **180 days of stay within a calendar year**, and it resets on
   1 January rather than on your visa anniversary.
2. Each individual visit is capped at **90 days** for most nationalities. Nationals of the United States, the United
   Kingdom, Canada and Japan get 180 days per visit instead.
3. **Any part of a day** in India counts as a full day, including arrival and departure days.
4. A stay crossing New Year is split across two allowances — days in December count against that year, days in January
   against the next. The per-visit limit doesn't split that way, since it measures the trip itself.
5. The 30-day visa works differently: it is **30 days from first arrival**, with multiple entries inside that period,
   and no annual-cap arithmetic at all.

Keep your electronic travel authorisation, entry and exit stamps, and boarding passes. Your day count against the
annual cap has to be reconstructable across multiple trips, which is far easier logged as you go.

## Edge cases

- **A 5-year visa is not a 5-year stay.** It's five years of being able to travel, with 180 days of actual presence
  available in each calendar year.
- **The reset is the calendar, not your visa date.** Someone who uses 180 days between July and December gets a fresh
  180 on 1 January, while someone who used them January to June waits until the following January.
- **Overland entry works only at named crossings.** Entry is restricted to designated points, overwhelmingly airports
  and seaports, with a short and periodically revised list of land crossings alongside them. Departure is far less
  restricted, so the risk sits entirely on the way in.
- **Some nationalities can't use the e-Visa at all** and need a regular visa from an Indian mission, and the eligible
  list changes.
- **Certain areas need additional permits regardless of your visa.** Protected and restricted areas require a separate
  permit that the e-Visa doesn't cover.

## If you get this rule wrong

Overstaying an Indian visa carries a fine that escalates with the length of the overstay. It has to be settled with the
Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) before you can leave, which is an exit-clearance process rather than a
payment at the airport. Longer overstays bring deportation and a blacklisting that blocks future visas, and both stay on
your immigration record. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A short holiday on the 30-day visa

You apply online, receive your authorisation, and fly into Delhi for a three-week trip. The 30-day visa covers it, and
multiple entries let you dip into Nepal and return within the same 30-day window.

### A visit that straddles New Year

You hold a 5-year visa and stay from early December to late January. The December days count against one calendar
year's 180 and the January days against the next, and the trip stays inside the 90-day limit on a single visit.

### Running out of allowance mid-year

You hold a 1-year visa and make three separate trips between January and June, totalling 180 days. Your visa remains
valid for months more, but you cannot re-enter until 1 January, when a fresh annual allowance begins.

## FAQ

### How long can I actually stay on a 5-year e-Tourist visa?

Up to 180 days in any calendar year. The five years describe how long the visa lets you keep travelling, not how long
any stay can be.

### When does the 180-day allowance reset?

On 1 January. It follows the calendar year rather than your visa's issue date, so a stay spanning New Year draws from
two separate annual allowances.

### How long can a single visit be?

Up to 90 days for most nationalities, and up to 180 days for nationals of the United States, the United Kingdom,
Canada and Japan. That limit applies to the trip itself, on top of the 180-day annual cap.

### Can I enter India overland on an e-Visa?

Only at a designated land crossing, and most land borders are not designated. Entry is otherwise restricted to named
airports and seaports, so check the current list against your route before relying on it.

### How many entries do I get?

Multiple entries on every current e-Tourist variant, including the 30-day one. What limits you is the stay itself —
the annual cap on the longer visas, and the separate limit on any single visit.

### Can I work on an e-Tourist visa?

No. It covers tourism, visiting friends and family, and certain short programmes. Employment, journalism, research and
missionary work all fall outside it and need different visa categories.

### Can I extend an e-Tourist visa?

No. The e-Visa is issued as non-extendable and non-convertible, so it can't be stretched or swapped for another
category from inside India. Staying longer means a different visa applied for in the normal way.
