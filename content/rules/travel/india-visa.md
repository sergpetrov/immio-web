# India e-Tourist Visa (180-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
India's e-Tourist visa comes in three lengths, and the one that catches people is the annual cap rather than the visa's
validity. The **30-day** version allows two entries. The **1-year** and **5-year** versions allow unlimited entries
across their validity, but limit you to **180 days in any calendar year** — so a five-year visa is five years of
travel, not five years of staying.
:::

| Key facts               |                                         |
|:------------------------|----------------------------------------:|
| Stay limit              |         180 days per calendar year |
| Period / Window         |          Calendar year (1 Jan – 31 Dec) |
| Alternative             |      30-day visa / 2 entries |
| Counting                |                       Any part of a day |
| Additional requirements | Designated entry points, no work |

## Understanding the rule

The three variants are genuinely different permissions rather than the same thing at different prices:

- **30-day e-Tourist visa** — valid 30 days from your first arrival, with two entries allowed inside that window.
- **1-year e-Tourist visa** — valid 365 days from the date it's granted, multiple entries, capped at 180 days of stay
  in any calendar year.
- **5-year e-Tourist visa** — valid 5 years from grant, multiple entries, the same 180-day annual cap.

The **calendar year** basis of that cap is what surprises people. It runs 1 January to 31 December regardless of when
your visa was issued, so the allowance resets at New Year rather than on your visa's anniversary. A stay straddling New
Year draws from two separate annual allowances.

The e-Visa is an authorisation obtained online before travel — you receive an electronic travel authorisation by email
and the actual visa is stamped on arrival. It permits tourism, visiting friends and relatives, short yoga or medical
programmes depending on the sub-category, and casual business meetings under the separate business e-Visa. It permits
no employment, no journalism, no research, and no missionary activity.

## How to keep track

1. On the 1-year and 5-year visas, the limit is **180 days of stay within a calendar year**, and it resets on
   1 January rather than on your visa anniversary.
2. **Any part of a day** in India counts as a full day, including arrival and departure days.
3. A stay crossing New Year is split across two allowances — days in December count against that year, days in January
   against the next.
4. The 30-day visa works differently: it is **30 days from first arrival**, with two entries allowed inside that
   period, and no annual-cap arithmetic at all.
5. Entry is only permitted through **designated airports and seaports**. Arriving overland at a land border is not
   possible on an e-Visa, though you can generally depart through any authorised point.

Keep your electronic travel authorisation, entry and exit stamps, and boarding passes. Your day count against the
annual cap has to be reconstructable across multiple trips, which is far easier logged as you go.

## Edge cases

- **A 5-year visa is not a 5-year stay.** It's five years of being able to travel, with 180 days of actual presence
  available in each calendar year.
- **The reset is the calendar, not your visa date.** Someone who uses 180 days between July and December gets a fresh
  180 on 1 January, while someone who used them January to June waits until the following January.
- **Land borders don't accept e-Visas.** You must arrive at one of the designated airports or seaports, which rules out
  overland entry from a neighbouring country on this route.
- **Some nationalities can't use the e-Visa at all** and need a regular visa from an Indian mission, and the eligible
  list changes.
- **Certain areas need additional permits regardless of your visa.** Protected and restricted areas require a separate
  permit that the e-Visa doesn't cover.

## If you get this rule wrong

Overstaying an Indian visa carries a fine that escalates with the length of the overstay, and it has to be settled with the Foreigners Regional Registration Office before you can leave — an exit-clearance process rather than a payment at the airport. Longer overstays bring deportation and a blacklisting that blocks future visas, and both the overstay and the blacklist stay on your immigration record. **Professional immigration advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A short holiday on the 30-day visa

You apply online, receive your authorisation, and fly into Delhi for a three-week trip. The 30-day visa covers it, and
the second permitted entry would let you dip into Nepal and return within the same 30 days.

### A long visa used across two years

You hold a 5-year visa and stay from November to March. The November and December days count against one calendar
year's 180, and the January to March days against the next — so neither allowance comes close to being exhausted.

### Running out of allowance mid-year

You hold a 1-year visa and spend January to June in India, reaching 180 days. Your visa remains valid for months more,
but you cannot re-enter until 1 January, when a fresh annual allowance begins.

## FAQ

### How long can I actually stay on a 5-year e-Tourist visa?

Up to 180 days in any calendar year. The five years describe how long the visa lets you keep travelling, not how long
any stay can be.

### When does the 180-day allowance reset?

On 1 January. It follows the calendar year rather than your visa's issue date, so a stay spanning New Year draws from
two separate annual allowances.

### Can I enter India overland on an e-Visa?

No. e-Visa holders must arrive at one of the designated airports or seaports. Departure is generally allowed through
any authorised point, but entry is restricted.

### How many entries do I get?

The 30-day visa allows two entries within its 30-day window. The 1-year and 5-year visas allow unlimited entries across
their validity, subject to the 180-day annual cap.

### Can I work on an e-Tourist visa?

No. It covers tourism, visiting friends and family, and certain short programmes. Employment, journalism, research and
missionary work all fall outside it and need different visa categories.

### Can I extend an e-Tourist visa?

No, it isn't extendable in the ordinary case. If you need longer than the visa allows, you leave and re-enter within
your remaining annual allowance, or apply for a different visa category.
