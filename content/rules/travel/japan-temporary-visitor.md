# Japan Temporary Visitor (90-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
Landing permission is normally granted for **90 days** under Japan's visa exemption, which covers nationals of
**70+ countries and regions**. It is **15 days** for Indonesia and Thailand and **30 days** for Brunei and Qatar. The
exemption covers **no income-earning activity** at all.
:::

| Key parameters          |                             |
|:------------------------|----------------------------:|
| Limit                   |                     90 days |
| Period / Window         |                   Per visit |
| Counting                |           Any part of a day |
| Alternative             |                 15, 30 days |
| Additional requirements | No paid work, passport type |

## Understanding the rule

This applies to nationals of the countries and regions Japan has reciprocal visa exemption arrangements with. What you
are granted on landing is the **Temporary Visitor** status: a genuine short stay — tourism, visiting family or
friends, meetings and similar business visits — and no work for payment, whoever pays it.

Several arrangements carry passport conditions, and they are the usual reason a traveler who thought they were exempt
is turned away:

- **Registration in advance** — nationals of Indonesia and Qatar are exempt only if their ePassport, compliant with
  International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) standards, has been registered with a Japanese diplomatic mission.
  The registration lasts 3 years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first.
- **ePassport required** — nationals of Brazil, Malaysia, Montenegro, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Serbia, Thailand and the
  United Arab Emirates.
- **Machine-readable passport required** — nationals of Barbados, Lesotho and Türkiye.
- **Document-specific arrangements** — Taiwan covers passports carrying a personal identification number, and Hong
  Kong and Macao cover specific Special Administrative Region (SAR) and British National Overseas passports.

The exemption removes the visa requirement, not the immigration check. An inspector still has to be satisfied that
what you are coming to do is a short-term stay.

## How to keep track

1. The period is the one **stamped on landing** — 90 days for most nationalities, 15 or 30 days for the few named
   above — and it runs per visit rather than as an annual allowance.
2. **Any part of a day** counts, so the day you land and the day you leave both sit inside the permitted period.
3. There is **no rolling window** in the exemption itself. Each entry is judged on its own, and the ceiling that
   matters is the date your landing permission expires.
4. Where an arrangement allows **up to 6 months**, the additional period is granted only on an
   **extension applied for before the permitted stay expires**, not at the airport.

Keep your landing permission sticker or stamp, your onward ticket and your accommodation details, and check your
passport type against the conditions for your nationality well before you fly rather than at the check-in desk.

## Edge cases

- **Income-earning activity is outside the exemption entirely.** The waiver is for a stay of 90 days or less where you
  will not be engaged in earning income, so paid work in Japan needs a different status, however short the job.
- **The 6-month arrangements still start at 90 days.** Nationals of the countries whose arrangements allow up to
  6 months are admitted first, then extend inside Japan, so the longer period is never automatic on arrival.
- **Registration-based exemptions expire.** For the arrangements that require prior registration of an ePassport, the
  registration runs 3 years or until the passport expires, and a renewed passport means registering again.
- **Being deported carries a long bar on returning.** Someone removed from Japan is barred from landing for 5 years,
  or 10 years if they have been removed or left under a departure order before.

## If you get this rule wrong

Overstaying makes you removable, and the bar on coming back is measured in years rather than months. It runs 5 years from the date of removal, or 10 years for anyone previously removed or previously departed under a departure order. Leaving under a departure order carries 1 year. Overstaying is also a criminal offense, punishable by imprisonment, a fine, or both. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A holiday inside the stamp

You fly into Narita on a passport from a 90-day country, are stamped in for 90 days, and travel for 5 weeks. Nothing
further is needed, and the exemption covers the whole trip.

### The 6-month arrangement, used properly

Your nationality is on one of the arrangements allowing up to 6 months. You are admitted for 90 days, then apply to
the Regional Immigration Bureau before that period runs out to extend the stay rather than leaving and re-entering.

### A short paid job that the exemption doesn't cover

You are offered 2 weeks of paid work in Tokyo during a 3-week visit. The stay is inside the exemption, the work is
not, because the waiver covers only stays where you are not engaged in earning income.

## FAQ

### How long can I stay in Japan without a visa?

90 days for most exempt nationalities, stamped on landing. Nationals of Indonesia and Thailand are admitted for
15 days and nationals of Brunei and Qatar for 30.

### Can I extend a 90-day visa-free stay in Japan?

Only where your nationality is on an arrangement allowing stays of up to 6 months. Then you apply to the Regional
Immigration Bureau before the permitted period expires rather than at the border.

### Can I work in Japan on the visa exemption?

No. The waiver applies to stays where you will not be engaged in income-earning activities, so any paid work needs a
status of residence obtained separately.

### Does my passport type matter for Japan's visa exemption?

For several nationalities, yes. Some arrangements require an ICAO-compliant ePassport, some require a machine-readable
passport, and two require the ePassport to be registered with a Japanese mission in advance.

### Is there a limit on how many times I can enter Japan visa-free?

The exemption sets no annual allowance and no rolling window. Each entry is assessed on its own, and an inspector has
to be satisfied that the visit really is a short-term stay.

### What happens if I overstay in Japan?

You become removable, and the bar on returning runs 5 years from removal, or 10 years if you have been removed or have
left under a departure order before. Leaving under a departure order carries a 1-year bar.
