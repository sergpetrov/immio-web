# Turkey Short Stay (90/180-day rule)

## Overview

:::callout
A visa or a visa exemption cannot give you **more than 90 days** of stay in Turkey within **any 180 days**, and the
180 days runs from your **first entry**. Staying longer means a **short-term residence permit**, applied for inside the country.
:::

| Key parameters          |                                  |
|:------------------------|---------------------------------:|
| Limit                   |                          90 days |
| Period / Window         |                 Rolling 180 days |
| Counting                |                Any part of a day |
| Additional requirements | Passport valid 60 days past stay |

## Understanding the rule

This applies to every visitor, whether you enter on a visa exemption, an e-Visa or a consular visa. The cap sits on
top of whichever document you hold. No visa or exemption can provide more than 90 days of stay within each 180 days,
and consulates cannot issue a visitor visa granting more in the first place.

Two pairs of numbers get confused constantly:

- **Validity against duration of stay** — an e-Visa's validity period is the window inside which you may enter Turkey.
  The duration of stay is how long you may remain once you have. The shorter one is what catches people out.
- **Stay against passport validity** — your passport has to stay valid for at least 60 days beyond the duration of
  stay of your visa, e-Visa, exemption or residence permit.

Anyone wanting to stay past 90 days applies for a short-term residence permit at the Provincial Directorate of
Migration Administration. That is a separate permission with its own conditions, not an extension of a visitor stay.

## How to keep track

1. The limit is **90 days of presence** inside a **180-day period that runs from your first entry**, not a fresh
   90 days for each arrival.
2. **Any part of a day** counts, so both the day you enter and the day you leave sit inside the total.
3. Once you have used the 90 days, the next entry on the same basis waits until the **180 days from that first entry**
   have run out.
4. The cap follows **you**, not your documents. Time spent on a second passport counts toward the same 90 days, and
   swapping between them changes nothing.

Keep your entry and exit stamps, your e-Visa printout, and boarding passes for every trip. The 180-day period runs
from a specific entry date, so knowing that date exactly matters more than remembering how many days each trip took.

## Edge cases

- **A second passport does not buy a second allowance.** Turkey states this directly: someone holding two passports
  cannot use each for 90 days within the same 180-day period.
- **Your nationality may allow less than 90 days.** The ceiling of 90 days in 180 is the maximum a visa or exemption
  can give, not a guarantee, and several nationalities are exempt only for a shorter stay.
- **e-Visa validity is not stay length.** You may enter at any point inside the validity period, but the duration of
  stay limits how long you remain. It is the shorter of the two figures.
- **e-Visas cover tourism and trade only.** Any other purpose needs a consular visa or a residence permit, whatever
  the day count says.
- **A residence permit can lapse through absence.** A residence permit is cancelled where the holder stays outside
  Turkey for more than 120 days in total during the last year, so moving onto a permit replaces one day count with
  another.

## If you get this rule wrong

An overstay is recorded against you and normally means an entry ban, with the length turning on how long you overstayed and on whether you left before the authorities found it. Leaving on your own initiative and paying the fine keeps the bans short — 1 month for an overstay of 3 to 6 months, rising to 5 years past 3 years. Being removed instead moves you onto a harsher scale, from 3 months up to 5 years. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A trip well inside the limit

You enter Istanbul in March on an e-Visa and stay 3 weeks, then return in May for another 2 weeks. Both visits sit
inside the same 180-day period and total 35 days, comfortably under the 90-day cap.

### Using the allowance up

You enter in January and stay 90 days, leaving in early April. The allowance is gone until the 180 days measured from
that January entry have run their course, so a return in May is not available on the same basis.

### The second passport that changes nothing

You hold two nationalities and enter on one passport for 60 days, then plan a further 60 on the other. The cap applies
to you rather than to each document, so the second trip would take you past 90 days within the same 180.

## FAQ

### How long can I stay in Turkey as a visitor?

Up to 90 days within any 180 days, whether you enter on a visa exemption, an e-Visa or a consular visa. No visitor
document can give more than that.

### When does my 180-day period start?

At your first entry. The e-Visa guidance is explicit about it: the 90 days are counted inside 180 days that run from
that first entry date, and a fresh application follows once those 180 days have passed.

### Can I use two passports to stay longer in Turkey?

No. The ministry states that someone holding two different passports cannot stay 90 days on each within the same
180-day period. The limit attaches to the person.

### Is my e-Visa validity the same as how long I can stay?

No, and this is the common mix-up. The validity period is the window in which you may enter; the duration of stay is
how long you may remain once you have entered.

### How do I stay in Turkey for more than 90 days?

By applying for a short-term residence permit at the Provincial Directorate of Migration Administration. It is a
separate permission decided on its own conditions, not an extension of a visitor stay.

### How long is the passport validity requirement for Turkey?

Your passport or travel document must be valid for at least 60 days beyond the duration of stay allowed by your visa,
e-Visa, exemption or residence permit.
