# United Arab Emirates Visit Visa (30-day and 90/180-day rules)

## Overview

:::callout
Entry to the UAE is free for many nationalities for **90 days** within **any 180-day** period, or **30 days**
per **visit**. **GCC nationals** need no visa at all, some nationalities can take a **visa on arrival** instead.
:::

| Key parameters          |                                       |
|:------------------------|--------------------------------------:|
| Limit                   |                               90 days |
| Period / Window         |                      Rolling 180 days |
| Counting                |                     Any part of a day |
| Alternative             |                     30 days per visit |
| Additional requirements | Nationality determines route, no work |

## Understanding the rule

There is no single UAE visitor rule — there are several parallel regimes, and which one you're in is decided before you
do anything, by your nationality:

- **GCC nationals** — Saudi, Kuwaiti, Bahraini, Omani and Qatari citizens enter without a visa, and can travel on a
  national identity card rather than a passport.
- **90 days within any 180** — nationals of European Union and Schengen states enter free of charge under reciprocal
  waivers, as do some others, including New Zealand passport holders.
- **30 days per visit** — many other nationalities are stamped in on arrival for a month at a time, counted per entry
  rather than across a window.
- **Short paid permissions** — a handful of other nationalities qualify through residence held elsewhere, such as in
  the United States, the United Kingdom or the European Union. They take a 14 or 60-day visa at the airport.
- **Pre-arranged visit visa** — everyone else applies in advance, usually sponsored by an airline, hotel, tour
  operator, relative or UAE company.

Whichever route applies, your passport needs at least six months left on it at the point you enter. And none of these
permissions carries any right to work. Employment requires a [residence visa](/rules/uae-residence-visa) with a work permit, sponsored by an
employer, which is an entirely separate process.

## How to keep track

1. Confirm which regime your nationality falls into first — 90-day, 30-day, visa on arrival, or pre-arranged
   — because everything else follows from that.
2. **Any part of a day** counts, so your arrival and departure days both fall inside the permitted period.
3. On a **90-day** entitlement, the 90 days are a shared allowance across every trip, not a limit that resets when
   you land. Count back 180 days from the date you care about, and total your days inside the UAE across that stretch.
4. On a **30-day** permission, count each visit on its own. These are usually granted fresh on each entry, so no
   window keeps running underneath.
5. Extensions are **granted at the authority's discretion**, normally for the same length as the permission you
   already hold, and they can be applied for more than once. Your **total stay cannot exceed 120 days**.
6. Don't plan around a grace period. Whether one applies depends on how you were admitted, and the daily fine starts
   once your permitted stay ends.

Keep your entry stamp, any visa approval, and extension receipts. Your permitted-until date comes from the stamp or
the visa, and the UAE's entry and exit records are fully digital, so a disputed count is settled from their system
rather than your recollection.

## Edge cases

- **90-day entitlements are an allowance, not a per-trip reset.** Leaving and returning doesn't restore a full 90
  days, since the 180-day window keeps running underneath.
- **A residence visa is a different thing entirely.** Visit permissions carry no work rights and don't lead anywhere —
  living or working in the UAE requires an employer-sponsored or investor residence visa.
- **The eligible nationality lists change.** The authorities say so themselves, and countries are added to the
  visa-free and visa-on-arrival lists periodically, so a list you checked a year ago may be out of date.
- **Border-running to reset a 30-day stay is well recognised.** A quick exit and re-entry to obtain a fresh stamp is a
  known pattern and can result in refusal at the counter rather than a new stamp.

## If you get this rule wrong

Overstaying accrues a fine for each day past your permitted date, charged the same way whether you came in as a
visitor, a tourist or a resident. It has to be cleared before you can leave, and a longer overstay adds an exit permit
with its own fee. Repeat or lengthy cases lead to deportation and a re-entry ban recorded against your passport, and
because entry records are fully digital, an unresolved overstay surfaces at every later border crossing and visa
application. **Professional advice is strongly recommended in situations like this.**

## Examples

### A visa-free business trip

You hold a passport on the 90-day list and fly to Dubai for a fortnight of meetings. You're stamped in at the border
at no cost, and the days you used stay on your record across the 180-day window for further trips.

### Extending a 30-day stay

You enter on a 30-day permission and want to stay longer. You apply from inside the UAE before it expires and, if the
authority approves, receive a further period of the same length. You can ask again later, but everything you're
granted has to fit inside a 120-day total.

### Assuming a fresh 90 days on re-entry

You spend 80 days in the UAE on a 90-day entitlement, fly out for a week, and return expecting a new 90. Because the
allowance runs across a 180-day window, you have around 10 days left rather than a fresh three months.

## FAQ

### How long can I stay in the UAE without a visa?

It depends on your nationality. GCC nationals need no visa at all. European Union and Schengen nationals, and some
others, get 90 days within any 180. Many other nationalities are stamped in for 30 days per visit, and everyone else
applies before traveling.

### Is a 90-day entitlement 90 days per trip?

No. It is 90 days within any 180-day period, shared across all your entries, so leaving and returning doesn't restore
a full 90. Dubai's residency authority publishes an official calculator that works out the remaining days from your
previous trips.

### How many times can I extend a visit visa?

There is no fixed number. The authority can extend a visit or tourist permission once or several times at its
discretion, normally for the same length as the permission you hold. The binding limit is that your total stay cannot
exceed 120 days.

### Is there a grace period after my visa expires?

Don't rely on one. What applies depends on how you were admitted, and the published position has changed over time.
Treat your permitted-until date as the deadline, because the daily fine is calculated from the day your stay ends.

### Can I work on a visit visa?

No. Employment requires a residence visa with a work permit, sponsored by a UAE employer. Visit permissions carry no
work rights of any kind.

### Does a visit visa lead to residence?

No. It's a separate track. Residence comes through employer sponsorship, investment, property, or one of the
longer-term visa categories, each applied for on its own terms.
